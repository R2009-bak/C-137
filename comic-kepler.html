Chapter 2 — The Hidden Shadow

The transit methodWhen a planet crosses in front of its star, the star dims a tiny bit. Dips that repeat give the planet's orbit; the depth of the dip hints at its size.
Drag the planet across the front of the star — watch the graph

I cannot see every planet. But I can see the shadow it leaves.

Kepler stared at about 150,000 stars at once, waiting for those tiny dips.

Chapter 3 — Another Earth?

Night after night, the dips added up.

Artist's concept — not a photograph

One small shadow. A whole new world of questions.

Kepler-186fAnnounced April 2014: the first Earth-size planet found in the habitable zone of another star. It circles its star every 130 days and gets about one-third of the energy Earth gets from the Sun. About 500 light-years away, in the constellation Cygnus.

Habitable zone = the distance where liquid water could exist. It does not mean life was found.

I can still see the stars. I just can't hold them still.

Two of Kepler's four wheels failed — the first in July 2012, the second in May 2013. It could no longer point precisely.

Chapter 5 — Second Light

Engineers on Earth found a fix: balance Kepler against the gentle push of sunlight.

2014: the K2 mission. Kepler now looked along the plane of the planets, switching fields every three months or so.

The light that pushed me away became the light that helped me stay.

Chapter 6 — One Last Look

Planned for 10 campaigns, K2 finished 19 — studying objects in our solar system, supernovae and supermassive black holes.
